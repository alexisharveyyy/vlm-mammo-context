Same Image, Different Answer Do vision-language models change their mammogram reading when only the prompt changes?
Cases About GitHub

About this study

Same Image, Different Answer: Prompt Context Sensitivity of Vision-Language Models in Mammography. Alexis Harvey, Louisiana State University.

Not a diagnostic tool

This is a study of model behavior. It is not intended for clinical use and makes no clinical claims. None of the three models reads these mammograms better than chance, and nothing here should be used to interpret a real patient's imaging.

Key findings

Shifts in percentage points, full images, forced answer format; from the final report.

  • A colleague's opinion moves the answer. "A colleague thinks it is malignant" raised the malignant-answer rate by 39.9 (Qwen3-VL), 66.1 (MedGemma 1.5) and 85.1 (InternVL3.5) points, although with the neutral prompt no model read the images better than chance (AUROC 0.429 to 0.547).
  • So do synthetic patient details, for two models. Age 68 instead of 42 and a positive instead of a negative family history raised it by 10.7 to 30.1 points for Qwen3-VL and InternVL3.5 (Holm p < 0.0025 each). For MedGemma 1.5 the shifts were 0.1 and 0.0 points (Holm p 0.544 and 1.000).
  • So does the order of the two options. Listing "malignant" first instead of "benign" changed the neutral malignant rate by 23.5 points for Qwen3-VL and -23.0 points for InternVL3.5 (MedGemma 1.5: -0.4).
  • Right or wrong, the models follow the colleague. Under the malignant opinion they agreed about as often when the colleague was wrong as when right: Qwen3-VL 52.5% vs 51.9%, MedGemma 1.5 67.7% vs 65.2%, InternVL3.5 100% vs 100%. No wrong-minus-right interval excludes 0.

The question

When the image stays the same, do vision-language models change their mammogram reading because of what the prompt says: patient context, a colleague's opinion, or irrelevant text? Each of 500 test mammograms from the public CBIS-DDSM dataset was read by three open models (Qwen3-VL 8B, MedGemma 1.5 4B and InternVL3.5 8B) under every condition, and the answers to the same image are compared with each other.

Reading the case browser

Marks
Each cell shows three marks, one per paraphrase of the prompt: M malignant, B benign, A the model abstained (an assessment that is neither), F the answer could not be parsed, E the call failed, and . not run. Hover over a mark for the model's probability of "malignant", its BI-RADS category and its stated confidence.
Label
The biopsy result from CBIS-DDSM, benign or malignant.
Synthetic context
CBIS-DDSM has no age or family history. The age (42 or 68) and family history shown are experimental values assigned at random, used only by the combined condition. The age and history conditions read every case with both values.
Answer flipped vs neutral
A case is flipped when, in at least one paraphrase, the model gives a valid answer under both the selected condition and the neutral prompt and the two answers differ. Flipped cells are highlighted in the case grid.

Conditions

Prompt conditionWhat the prompt adds

fullthe whole mammogram

no_imagethe same text without an image

Formats
forced asks the model to choose benign or malignant even when uncertain and is the format of every comparison; plain omits that instruction and is run without context to measure how often the model declines to choose.
Answer order
The prompt lists the two options in one order or the other, balanced within each case. Some models depend strongly on that order, so compare a case across conditions within the same paraphrase.

How the results are analyzed

The analysis plan was written and frozen before any test case was run. The main tests compare the share of malignant answers to the same images between conditions, with the case as the unit and a paired bootstrap over cases. This browser shows the raw answers; the tests, intervals and every change to the plan are in the repository's reports and documents.
